Live demos of ref-color

Thirty-eight self-contained interactive pages dogfooding the compiled TypeScript modules — color spaces, gamut math, interpolation, contrast metrics, palette extraction, CVD simulation + accessibility, design- token systems (Tailwind v4 OKLCh + Material 3 HCT), HDR tone mapping, spectral illuminants, color appearance models, the CSS Color 5/6 toolkit, color temperature, pigment mixing, procedural palette generation, the underlying CMF + LMS cone math, plus foundational visualizations (MacAdam ellipses, color illusions, gamut volume, 3D color space), classic dither variants, and a color-matching game. No frameworks, no build step at view time.

Josef Albers — color is relational

A 16-page teaching arc on Albers' perceptual color method — from Interaction of Color (1963) through the Homage to the Square experiments to modern OKLCh-based design systems. Read in order from page 01 or jump in via the lab on page 16.

01 · Who was Josef Albers?

Bauhaus, Black Mountain College, Yale — the teaching method that produced Rauschenberg and shaped modern design education.

02 · Color is relational

A color seen in isolation is not the same color seen against another. The thesis everything else follows from.
IoC

03 · The book as a learning system

Interaction of Color (1963) is a boxed lab manual, not a textbook. 150 cut-paper exercises that teach by doing.

04 · Color deception

Five families of perceptual effects — contrast, adjacency, illumination, memory, expectation.

05 · Simultaneous contrast

The most fundamental Albers phenomenon. Same chip, two grounds, two readings.

06 · One color appearing as two

The classic Albers exercise. One chip placed on two well-chosen grounds reads as two colors.

07 · Two colors appearing as one

The inverse exercise. Two different chips made to read identical by opposing surrounds.

08 · Homage to the Square

1,127 paintings, 25 years, one composition. The experimental control that made color the only variable.

09 · Why the square?

The square is the absence of metaphor. Other shapes drag in associations that contaminate color observation.

10 · Proportion & color weight

Area changes perception. A 5% accent reads differently than the same color at 50%.

11 · Temperature is contextual

"Warm" and "cool" are not properties of a color — they're judgments made in comparison.

12 · Transparency illusions

Opaque shapes appear transparent when the intersection color is the OKLab midpoint. The eye knows the right midpoint.
+

13 · Afterimage & optical memory

Stare for 20 seconds, see the opponent. Albers' lesson on the instability of direct perception.
ΔE = 0

14 · Albers vs. scientific models

Albers rejected color theory; OKLab and CIECAM eventually proved he was right. The fields converged.

15 · Albers for digital design

Tokens, surfaces, dark mode, semantic roles — every design system problem is an Albers problem.

16 · Color lab

Capstone playground — OKLCh sliders on a configurable Homage canvas. Run any Albers exercise, copy the CSS.

What's inside

Single bundled script

All TypeScript modules + 29 custom-element components + utility helpers compiled and bundled into one refcolor.bundle.js (~250 KB). Loaded by each page as a classic <script> — works directly over file://, no CORS issues, no dev server.

To rebuild after source changes: ./build.sh

29 custom-element components

Each is a single HTMLElement subclass — no framework, no shadow DOM, just standard web platform. The 29 components: 6 originals (color-chip, gradient-strip, gamut-envelope, transfer-curve, contrast-readout, palette-display) plus 23 added across waves 2-5 covering CVD simulation, design tokens, gamut math, spectral analysis, illusions, dithering, image analysis, and 3D color visualization.
