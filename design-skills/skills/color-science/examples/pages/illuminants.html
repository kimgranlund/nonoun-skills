Spectral illuminants

Every light source has a spectral power distribution — how much power it emits at each visible wavelength. Each illuminant has a characteristic SPD shape that, integrated against the CIE 1931 color matching functions, gives its XYZ chromaticity. The grid below renders the same 8 reflectance spectra under each illuminant — same physical surface, different reflected color.

Illuminants to compare

Why D65 is the web's default

D65 approximates noon daylight (~6500K) and is the reference for sRGB, Display P3, Rec.709, Rec.2020 — every display- facing RGB space the web uses. Its SPD is smoothly continuous, with a slight blue bias compared to D50.

D50 is warmer daylight (~5000K) — the ICC Profile Connection Space white, used in print workflows. Adobe RGB and ProPhoto are also D50-native.

A is incandescent tungsten — a roughly black-body Planckian source. Its SPD slopes heavily toward red, which is why photos shot under tungsten look orange without white-balancing.

F2 (and the F-series in general) are fluorescent lamps — their SPDs have visible spikes at mercury vapor emission lines. The bumps are why fluorescent-lit photos often look slightly green and have poor color rendering for skin tones.
