The central claim: color is relational

A color seen in isolation is not the same color as that color seen next to another. The eye reports context-dependent appearance, never raw reflectance. Everything else in Albers' teaching follows from this.

Why this is the whole game

If a color's appearance depends on its surround, then specifying a color by its hex value alone is half a specification. The other half is everything around it — and that "everything else" includes the next color over, lighting, screen calibration, your visual adaptation, even your memory of what color you expected to see.

Albers' entire teaching method follows from accepting this: stop measuring colors in isolation, and start practicing perception in composition. Every exercise that follows in his book is a controlled experiment in that direction.

Takeaway

  • Artists: never trust a chip outside its final composition.
  • Designers: a token's hex is half its color; the other half is context.
  • UI engineers: pick color on the actual UI surface, never in a default canvas.
