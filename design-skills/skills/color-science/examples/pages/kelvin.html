Color temperature (Planckian locus)

A blackbody at temperature T emits a unique spectrum, which integrates to a specific point on the CIE xy chromaticity diagram. As T sweeps from cool (1500 K, deep red) to hot (15000 K, blue-white), the chromaticity traces a curve called the Planckian locus. The "color temperature" of a white light source is the temperature of the blackbody whose chromaticity is closest.

6500 K

About the math

This page uses Kim et al. (2002) polynomial fits to the Planckian locus on CIE xy. The full physical derivation would integrate Planck's blackbody law B(λ, T) = 2hc² / λ⁵ · 1 / (exp(hc/λkT) − 1) against the CIE 1931 2° CMFs at 1nm steps. The Kim polynomial is accurate to within ~10⁻⁴ in (x, y) over 1667–25000 K — well below the MacAdam JND ellipses.

The reference dots show CIE standard illuminants A, D50, D55, D65, D75. They aren't exactly on the Planckian locus — daylight illuminants (the D series) are slightly off because actual daylight isn't a pure blackbody (atmospheric scattering, etc.). The distance from the locus is called Duv.
