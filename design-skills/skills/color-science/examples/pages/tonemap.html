HDR tone mapping

Light in the real world (and HDR captures) has unbounded intensity, but sRGB display values must fit in [0, 1]. Tone-mapping operators are functions that map [0, ∞) → [0, 1] smoothly. Each takes a different position on the trade-off between simplicity, highlight preservation, and saturation. The strips below show the same synthetic HDR gradient through five operators.

What each operator preserves

  • Naive clip — everything > 1 becomes 1. Blown highlights. The HDR information is lost.
  • Reinhard simple — x / (1 + x). Cheap, monotonic, never reaches 1.0 (asymptotic). Slight wash-out at SDR white.
  • Reinhard extended — maps your chosen white-point W exactly to 1.0. Better highlight definition.
  • Reinhard luminance-preserving — compress luminance only; scale RGB by the gain. Preserves hue (channels keep their ratios) but the result is more saturated.
  • ACES Narkowicz — the cinema/games industry default. Filmic toe (gentle shadow lift) + smooth highlight roll-off. Slightly desaturates shadows.
