Perceptual hue wheel

Three concentric hue rings at the same lightness, plotted in OKLCh (outer), HCT (middle), and HSL (inner). Look at any fixed angle: OKLCh shows uniform perceptual lightness around the wheel; HSL has visibly brighter greens and dimmer blues; HCT lands in between. The stripe of darkness across HSL's blue region is the canonical reason OKLCh became the default in CSS Color 4.

Controls

0.65 0.15

What to look for

  • Pick any fixed angle (say 240°, blue territory). Compare the three rings at that angle. OKLCh's blue should feel the same "brightness" as its red or its green. HSL's blue will look markedly darker.
  • Sweep the lightness slider toward extremes. At L=0.9, OKLCh's ring stays as a fully-vivid pastel — HSL's collapses near white for almost every hue.
  • Increase the OKLCh chroma slider until the OKLCh ring shows "spike-outs" — those are hues where the gamut runs out before your requested chroma. The ring is being soft-clipped to sRGB.
