Image chromaticity scatter

The "gamut analysis" technique photographers use to read an image. Drop an image and every pixel becomes a dot on the CIE 1931 xy chromaticity diagram, rendered in its actual color. The resulting cloud reveals the image's color signature: tightly clustered = narrow palette; spread toward the locus = saturated colors; centered = neutral/muted; one-sided = warm or cool bias.

Source

What the cloud shape tells you

  • Tight cluster around D65 (0.31, 0.33) — mostly neutral / desaturated image; not using much of the gamut.
  • Cloud extending toward the spectral locus — the image contains saturated colors close to the edge of human vision (and beyond sRGB).
  • Linear streak through a hue direction — strong single-hue dominance (sunsets, monochrome scenes, etc.).
  • L-shape or fork — two dominant hue families with a connecting neutral.

Combined with the hue + lightness histogram, scatter is one of the most informative ways to "read" an image's color from a math angle.
