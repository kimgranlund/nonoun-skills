Material 3 tonal palette generator

From a single source color, generate the five Material Design 3 tonal palettes — primary, secondary, tertiary, neutral, and neutral-variant. Each is rendered at 13 tones (CIELAB L*). The chroma is reduced at each tone if needed to stay in the sRGB gamut.

Why HCT instead of OKLCh?

Material 3 uses HCT (Hue + Chroma + Tone) for tonal palette generation. The key difference vs OKLCh: HCT's Tone is CIELAB L*, not OKLab L. The reasoning is anchor-compatibility — Material 3 wants "Tone 50" to mean the same perceptual lightness it did in CIELAB-based Material 2, even if a newer space is used for hue & chroma.

In practice the differences between HCT and OKLCh ramps are subtle. Tailwind v4 picked OKLCh (cleaner math, native CSS support); Material 3 picked HCT (back-compat with their existing systems and Munsell-friendly Tone axis). Both are perceptually much better than HSL-based ramps.

Try the red preset and look at the primary palette: the high-tone slots (90, 95) show chroma reduction — the requested chroma at L*=90 isn't reachable in sRGB at red's hue, so the algorithm reduces C until in-gamut. Same thing happens at low tones.
