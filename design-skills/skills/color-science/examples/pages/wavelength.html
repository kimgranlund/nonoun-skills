Most spectral colors are out of gamut

The sRGB gamut is the triangle whose three corners are sRGB's primaries — sitting inside the spectral locus. Pure monochromatic light is on the boundary of human vision; sRGB can only reproduce mixtures (combinations of the three primaries). What you see on screen is an approximation of pure spectral light, with much of the saturation lost.

Wider-gamut displays (Display P3, Rec.2020) cover more — but never all — of the locus. The pure-spectral red and violet wavelengths are out of even Rec.2020.
