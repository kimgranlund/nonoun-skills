Why each strategy looks the way it does

Naive clip just clamps each linear-RGB channel to [0, 1]. Cheap and wrong — the hue shifts (a vivid red becomes orange). Engines that "just do min(1, max(0, x))" are doing this.

CSS Color 4 reduces chroma in OKLCh, keeping the L axis and hue exactly. It's the W3C-normative algorithm and what color-mix() + display-p3 fallbacks use under the hood. Hue stable, lightness stable, chroma reduced as little as needed.

Min-ΔE blend reduces chroma and nudges L toward the gamut cusp's lightness — useful when the source is so vivid that pure chroma reduction would land on a depressing pastel. The lightness shift trades a tiny perceptual error for visibly more saturation.

Cusp projection takes the straight line from source to cusp and walks it inward until it hits the gamut boundary. This is Ottosson's "hue-dependent single-point projection." Hue stays exact; L and C both shift; pretty good results for very out-of-gamut inputs.
