Albers for digital product design

Tokens, surface systems, dark mode, contrast pairs, theming — every modern design-system problem is an Albers problem. The same brand-500 reads differently on the page background / on a card / next to an accent / in dark mode. Systems that ignore this ship colors that "look wrong everywhere except the spec."

What the tour shows

The brand-on-brand cell is the cautionary tale: the dot disappears because it's identical to its surround. The brand-on-error and brand-on-warning cells show how a competing hue dominates the perception. Even between the two "neutral" backgrounds — pure white vs. tinted lavender — the same dot reads with subtly different temperature.

The fix isn't more color tokens — it's contextual tokens. Modern design systems define brand-500 as the canonical value, then define semantic roles (action / on-action / surface-action / etc.) that may resolve to different hex values depending on what they sit against. Material 3 calls these "tonal palettes" and pairs every role with its readable counterpart. Tailwind v4 leans into OKLCh so the palette stops are perceptually even regardless of hue.

What to bring back to your team

  1. Pick brand colors last — after the surface system is locked.
  2. Test tokens on real screens, not isolated chips. (Build a "compose any token on any surface" sandbox.)
  3. Define semantic roles, not just brand-100 through brand-900. The hex value of "action color" depends on "what surface is action sitting on?"
  4. Audit dark mode with the same exercises. A token that passes in light mode often fails in dark — same Albers problem, different surround.
  5. Use OKLCh and color-mix(in oklab, ...) for derivations. Both produce results closer to what the eye expects.

Takeaway

  • Artists: your skills with paper translate directly to product work.
  • Designers: stop testing tokens on isolated chips; test on real layouts.
  • UI engineers: build a "compose any token on any surface" review tool — the only way to catch context drift before ship.
