Color volume comparison

How much bigger is Display P3 than sRGB? Adobe RGB? Rec.2020? The CIE xy chromaticity diagram shows triangle area — but that's only two dimensions. The actual gamut is a 3D solid (chromaticity × lightness), and "color volume" is the perceptually-meaningful measure.

Computed via Monte Carlo integration in OKLab — a perceptually-uniform space, so equal volume there really does correspond to equal perceived range. Result is normalized so sRGB = 1.0×.

Some intuitions to confirm

  • P3 / sRGB ≈ 1.3× — P3 is about 25-35% wider than sRGB in perceptual volume. Mostly extra reds and greens, with marginal blue gain.
  • Adobe RGB / sRGB ≈ 1.3-1.4× — similar total volume to P3, but the extra space is concentrated in greens (which is why Adobe RGB is favored for print/landscape work).
  • Rec.2020 / sRGB ≈ 1.7× — substantially wider in all three primary directions.
  • ProPhoto / sRGB > 1.9× — but most of the extra is "imaginary" colors (outside the spectral locus) that no display can reproduce. Useful as a working space, not a display space.

Why Monte Carlo?

Each gamut is the image of the linear-RGB unit cube under a 3×3 matrix transform — a parallelepiped in XYZ. Then the OKLab map is a nonlinear transform (cube root) on top of that, so the gamut becomes a curved 3D region. Computing its exact volume requires integrating the Jacobian determinant of the OKLab map over the cube, which is analytically painful.

Monte Carlo trades exactness for code simplicity: sample N points uniformly in an OKLab bounding box, test each for cube membership via the gamut's XYZ→linear-RGB matrix, multiply the in-gamut fraction by the box volume. With 30k samples the standard error is well under 1%.
