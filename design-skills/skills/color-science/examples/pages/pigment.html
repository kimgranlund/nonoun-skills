Kubelka-Munk paint mixer

Real paint doesn't mix by averaging RGB values — it mixes by physical absorption and scattering. Blue paint plus yellow paint really makes green, not a muddy gray. Kubelka-Munk single-constant mixing is the standard model: each pigment is a reflectance spectrum, mixing is linear in K/S space, and the result converts back to a believable color.

Why pigment mixing isn't averaging

Light-source mixing (your monitor) is additive — emitted photons sum. Paint mixing is subtractive — each pigment absorbs some wavelengths and reflects others, and the mix absorbs both. Blue paint absorbs everything except blue; yellow paint absorbs everything except yellow-green-red; together they absorb almost everything except green — which is what your eye sees as the mixed paint.

Try mixing White + black at 50/50. The Kubelka-Munk mid-gray is very dark (R ≈ 0.07) — not the 50% gray that RGB averaging gives (R ≈ 0.5). This is the key K-M property: the mixed reflectance is much closer to the darker pigment because the absorption ratios blend nonlinearly.
