Floyd-Steinberg dithering

Drop an image, quantize it to a small palette via k-means in OKLab, and see two versions side-by-side: nearest-neighbor mapping (no dithering — banding visible) and Floyd-Steinberg error diffusion (banding broken up into stippling). The dithered image uses the same palette but distributes quantization error to neighboring pixels.

How error diffusion works

When the nearest palette color isn't perfect, FS pushes the quantization error (source - palette) into neighboring pixels — 7/16 to the right, 3/16 down-left, 5/16 down, 1/16 down-right. The errors accumulate and eventually trigger compensating palette picks. The visual result averages out to the original tone, but with characteristic stippling.

In this implementation the nearest-neighbor metric is Euclidean OKLab, not Euclidean RGB. That means "closest to the eye" rather than "closest in 8-bit byte values" — produces noticeably better palettes on natural images, especially in low-k regimes.
