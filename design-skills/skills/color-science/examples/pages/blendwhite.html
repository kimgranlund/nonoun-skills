Blend through white

The canonical OKLab demo from Ottosson's 2020 article. Pick a vivid blue and watch what happens when you blend it toward white in five different color spaces. Encoded sRGB and HSL famously shift the hue toward purple — the "Photoshop blue → purple" bug. OKLab keeps the hue stable; CIELAB gets close but still drifts on saturated blues.

What you're seeing

Encoded sRGB: linear interpolation of byte values. The mid-tones are way too dark because encoded values aren't physically additive — averaging encoded 0.5 + 0.5 gives 0.5, but that's only ~0.21 in linear light. The vivid blue → white gradient has muddy purple in the middle.

Linear sRGB: decoded values are interpolated. Mid- tones are correct in luminance, but the path through RGB space doesn't follow the perceptual hue arc — blue → white drifts through cyan-ish or purple-ish territory.

OKLab: equal t-steps look like equal perceived steps. The hue stays put. This is why CSS Color 4's color-mix(in oklab, ...) picked OKLab as the default mixing space.

CIELAB: the predecessor; mostly fine but blues show a slight hue shift toward purple, which is the original motivation for OKLab.

HSL: mixing in cylindrical-RGB collapses chroma to 0 at the lightness midpoint (hue is undefined for the white anchor), producing the well-known "gray middle" gradient artifact.
