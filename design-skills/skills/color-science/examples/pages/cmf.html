What CMF and LMS represent

The CMF (color matching functions) are tabulated from the CIE 1931 experiment: a panel of observers mixed three primary lights to match a test monochromatic light at each wavelength. The amount of each primary needed becomes the x̄, ȳ, z̄ values. They're a mathematical description of color matching, not directly tied to cones.

The LMS cone fundamentals are the actual spectral sensitivities of the human L (long, red), M (medium, green), and S (short, blue) cones — derived from physiological data. They peak near 564, 534, and 420 nm respectively. Note that L and M overlap heavily (~30nm apart) which is why CVDs that lose one are so common.

The relationship is a linear transformation. The Ottosson M1 matrix shown here is one choice of LMS basis (used in OKLab). The CAT16 matrix in CIECAM16 is another. Smith-Pokorny and Stockman-Sharpe are yet others — each derived from a slightly different observer population.
