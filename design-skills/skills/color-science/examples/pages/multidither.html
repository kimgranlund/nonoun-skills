Multi-dither comparison

Quantize an image to k colors via k-means in OKLab, then apply four different dithering algorithms with the same palette. Compare the characteristic textures: Floyd-Steinberg's fine grain, Atkinson's punchier high-frequency look, Bayer's regular cross-hatch, Jarvis-Judice-Ninke's smoother but coarser result.

Inputs

How each algorithm differs

  • Floyd-Steinberg (1976) — diffuses error to 4 neighbors with weights 7/16, 3/16, 5/16, 1/16. The most popular algorithm. Produces fine-grain stippling.
  • Atkinson (1980s, Bill Atkinson at Apple) — diffuses 1/8 of error to 6 neighbors, propagating only 3/4 of the total error. Faster than FS; brighter / punchier output but can blow out very dark or very bright areas.
  • Bayer 4×4 ordered (1973) — adds a fixed 16-level pattern matrix to each pixel before nearest-neighbor assignment. No error propagation; pixels are independent. Characteristic cross-hatch. Good for animation (neighboring frames don't shimmer).
  • Jarvis-Judice-Ninke (1976) — diffuses to 12 neighbors with /48 weights. The biggest kernel; smoother textures but more visible "staircase" artifacts on hard edges.
