Perceptual uniformity, in numbers

A gradient is "perceptually uniform" iff ΔE_ok between consecutive stops is roughly constant. Lower variance = smoother visually-equal steps.

Why this matters

Linear interpolation in encoded sRGB produces noticeably darker mid-tones — the classic "muddy gradient" bug. The encoded values aren't physically additive: averaging encoded 0.5 + 0.5 gives 0.5, but that's only ~0.21 in linear light. The eye sees the encoded mid-tone as much darker than either endpoint.

Interpolating in OKLab is the modern default (CSS Color 4's color-mix(in oklab, ...)). It's perceptually uniform — equal t steps look like equal perceived steps.

OKLCh interpolation additionally lets you control the hue path. Try changing the "Hue path" dropdown above and watch the OKLCh strip change while the others stay fixed.
