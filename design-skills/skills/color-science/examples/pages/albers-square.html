Why the square?

The square has no direction, no story, no figure. It removes representation, gesture, narrative. What's left is pure color behavior — exactly what Albers wanted his students to observe. Compare the same colors arranged as squares, circles, and petals: the first reads as color; the others start to mean something else.

Same colors, three shape grammars

squares — pure color behavior
circles — reads as a target / portal
petals — reads as a flower / face

The square is the absence of metaphor

Squares share four equal sides at 90° — no horizontal or vertical preference, no "up", no implied motion. When you nest squares inside squares, the eye has nothing to interpret except the colors. That's what Albers wanted. The square is invisibility for the form, so the color becomes visible.

Circles introduce associations (the sun, the eye, a target). Irregular shapes invite figurative reading (a flower, a face, a creature). Even rectangles bring directional bias — landscape vs. portrait, horizon vs. tower. The square alone is mute.

Takeaway

  • Artists: strip narrative; isolate the phenomenon you're studying.
  • Designers: prototype with neutral primitives before stylizing — the color system tells the truth without the story.
  • UI engineers: token playgrounds should use square chips; rounded "production" components hide color failures.
