MacAdam ellipses

The single most important visualization for understanding why CIE xy is not a perceptual color space. David MacAdam's 1942 experiment found that the "just-noticeable difference" (JND) regions around 25 reference colors are massively non-uniform — green ellipses are roughly 10× larger than blue ellipses in (x, y) units, meaning green chromaticities need much bigger numerical differences to be perceptually distinguishable.

Ellipse scale

10× (canonical)

The big idea

The ellipses are tiny at scale 1× — invisible to the eye on a printed chart. The canonical visualization scales them up 10× so you can see their shapes and sizes. At 10×, the disparity between blue ellipses (cramped near the bottom of the locus) and green ellipses (large in the upper middle) is unmistakable.

Why this matters: Euclidean distance in CIE xy doesn't correspond to perceptual difference. A change of (Δx=0.01, Δy=0) feels like a tiny shift in blue but a giant shift in green. CIELAB (1976) and OKLab (2020) exist specifically to fix this — they're designed so that the JND ellipses become circles of roughly uniform size.

Open the ΔE comparator for an alternate angle on the same problem: each ΔE metric is essentially a rule for "how far is far" in some color space; the metrics differ in how they correct for non-uniformity.
