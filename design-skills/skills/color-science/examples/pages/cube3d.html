3D sRGB cube in perceptual space

The sRGB color cube is a perfect [0, 1]³ cube — black at one corner, white at the opposite, primaries at the other six. But when you embed it into a perceptual color space like OKLab or CIELAB, the cube distorts. The corners stretch, the faces curl, because the eye doesn't see linear RGB linearly. What the distortion shows

In the OKLab view, the black corner is at the bottom, white at the top, and the six primaries spread around the equator at varying "altitudes" (L values). Look at where pure blue (#0000ff) lands — it's at L ≈ 0.45, well below the equator. Pure yellow (#ffff00) is near L ≈ 0.97, almost at white-level. Yet both have RGB byte sum = 255, so they're "equally bright" by RGB byte measures.

This is the canonical reason linear-RGB averaging produces wrong mid-tones — the cube embedded in perceptual space isn't straight, so a linear interpolation between any two corners curves through perceptually-wrong territory. Mixing in OKLab follows the straight line in OKLab, which is the curved (correct) line in sRGB.
