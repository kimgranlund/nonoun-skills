OKHSL picker — perceptual HSL

OKHSL is Björn Ottosson's perceptual replacement for HSL (2021). Same cylindrical (h, s, l) interface, but built on OKLab with gamut-aware saturation. Compare the slices below at the same hue: in OKHSL the horizontal "lightness axis" feels uniformly bright across all saturations; in HSL the saturated row is markedly darker than the desaturated row.

What makes OKHSL different

A naïve "perceptual HSL" fails because the sRGB gamut isn't a cylinder in OKLab — at fixed hue, peak chroma varies dramatically by lightness (peaks roughly at the cusp). OKHSL solves this by parameterizing saturation as a normalized fraction of the cusp-shaped gamut triangle: s = 0 is gray, s = 1 is the boundary, and 0–1 is the perceptually-uniform interior. Lightness similarly maps the L axis relative to the cusp.

Practical implication: you can ship an HSL-style picker that designers already understand, but every (h, s, l) combination is visually meaningful — no "dark zone" at high saturations in the blue/purple hue range.
