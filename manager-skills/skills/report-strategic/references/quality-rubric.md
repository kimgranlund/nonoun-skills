# report-strategic: Quality Rubric

Score each dimension 0–3 before delivering. Target: ≥ 24 / 30 for publication-grade work.

## Scoring scale
- **3 — Excellent**: Dimension fully satisfied; no obvious improvements
- **2 — Good**: Mostly satisfied with minor gaps
- **1 — Partial**: Significant gaps but something present
- **0 — Missing**: Dimension not addressed

---

## Dimensions

### 1. Executive summary independence (0–3)
Can a reader who reads only the executive summary understand the situation, key findings,
and primary recommendation without needing to reference the body?

*3*: All three elements complete; no undefined terms; no forward references
*2*: All elements present but one relies on body context
*1*: Missing one of: situation / findings / recommendation
*0*: Executive summary is an introduction, not a summary

---

### 2. Vocabulary completeness (0–3)
Are all domain-specific terms, abbreviations, and contested terms defined in the glossary
before they are used in findings?

*3*: All terms defined; contested terms flagged with declared meaning
*2*: Most terms defined; 1–2 used without definition
*1*: Glossary present but incomplete (>3 undefined terms)
*0*: No glossary; terms used without definition

---

### 3. Scope clarity (0–3)
Is the scope of the report explicitly bounded? Are out-of-scope items named?

*3*: In-scope and out-of-scope both stated explicitly
*2*: In-scope stated; out-of-scope implicit
*1*: Vague scope statement only
*0*: No scope statement

---

### 4. Evidence quality (0–3)
Are findings anchored to evidence with source tiers noted?

*3*: Every finding cites evidence with tier (Primary/Secondary/Estimated)
*2*: Most findings cite evidence; 1–2 unsupported
*1*: Evidence present but tiers not noted; some findings unsupported
*0*: No evidence citations

---

### 5. Finding specificity (0–3)
Are findings specific and falsifiable, or vague and general?

*3*: Every finding is a specific, measurable, testable claim
*2*: Most findings specific; 1–2 general
*1*: Half or more are general observations
*0*: All findings are generic commentary

---

### 6. Implications coverage (0–3)
Do the implications connect findings to risks and opportunities for the reader's context?

*3*: Risks and opportunities both present; each linked to a specific finding
*2*: Both present but not all linked to specific findings
*1*: Only one of risks/opportunities addressed
*0*: No implications section

---

### 7. Recommendation actionability (0–3)
Can the reader act on the recommendations? Do they name action + owner + success condition?

*3*: Every recommendation: action verb + owner + success condition + timeline
*2*: Most recommendations complete; 1–2 missing an element
*1*: Recommendations present but not actionable (too vague or no owners)
*0*: No recommendations, or only "consider X" language

---

### 8. No orphaned findings (0–3)
Does every finding connect to at least one implication or recommendation?

*3*: Every finding feeds an implication or recommendation
*2*: 1 orphaned finding
*1*: 2–3 orphaned findings
*0*: Findings and recommendations appear to be independent sections

---

### 9. Visual clarity (0–3)
Are tables and diagrams used effectively? Do they have captions?

*3*: Tables/diagrams used where comparison or positioning analysis is present; all captioned
*2*: Used but 1–2 missing captions; or one obvious table opportunity missed
*1*: Minimal visuals; dense prose for data that should be tabular
*0*: No visuals in a report with comparative data

---

### 10. References completeness (0–3)
Are all sources cited in the body listed in the references section?

*3*: All sources listed; reliability tier noted for each
*2*: All sources listed; tiers not noted
*1*: Some sources missing from references
*0*: No references section

---

## Scoring interpretation

| Score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 27–30 | Publication-grade — ready to share |
| 22–26 | Near-ready — address specific gaps before sharing |
| 16–21 | Draft quality — significant work needed |
| <16 | Incomplete — continue developing before review |

## Common failure patterns

**High evidence scores, low implication scores**: The report describes what is, but
doesn't tell the reader what to do about it. Add implications and recommendations.

**High implication scores, low evidence scores**: Conclusions unsupported by evidence.
Add citations or downgrade confidence language.

**Low vocabulary scores with high finding scores**: Technical readers will follow;
non-specialist readers will be lost. Add the glossary.

**High recommendation scores, low orphaned-findings score**: Recommendations appear
to come from nowhere. Link each recommendation to specific findings.
